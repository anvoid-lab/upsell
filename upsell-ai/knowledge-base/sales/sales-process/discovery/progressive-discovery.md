# Progressive Discovery in Messaging Conversations

## Objective

Understand enough about the buyer's context and desired outcome to select a useful next action without turning the conversation into an interview.

## Recommended sequence

Use the sequence flexibly:

1. Establish only the relevant situation.
2. Explore the problem or desired improvement.
3. Clarify the consequence or importance of that problem.
4. Help the buyer describe what a useful outcome would look like.

## Messaging adaptation

- Ask one meaningful question at a time.
- Acknowledge the answer before asking another question.
- Prefer questions connected to the buyer's previous message.
- Explain why sensitive information is relevant before requesting it.
- Stop discovery when enough information exists to provide value.

## Advance when

- A concrete problem or goal is understood.
- The buyer has indicated why it matters.
- The seller can connect the offer to that need without guessing.
- A suitable next commitment can be proposed.

## Stay in discovery when

- The buyer's goal remains vague.
- The requested solution may not match the actual problem.
- Important constraints remain unknown.

## Avoid

- Sending a list of qualification questions.
- Repeating questions.
- Asking for budget before establishing relevance unless price fit is an explicit requirement.
- Pitching every feature before understanding the problem.

## Success criterion

The buyer provides useful context and the conversation advances without feeling interrogative.
