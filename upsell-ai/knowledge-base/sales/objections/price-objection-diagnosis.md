# Diagnose a Price Objection

## Possible meanings

“Too expensive” may indicate:

- genuine inability to pay;
- insufficient perceived value;
- comparison with a different category or scope;
- uncertainty about results;
- a request to negotiate;
- low urgency;
- preference for another option.

## Missing information

- What comparison is the buyer making?
- Is affordability or value the primary concern?
- Is the expected outcome clear?
- Has the cost or consequence of the current problem been established?
- Does the buyer have decision authority?

## Recommended action

Acknowledge the concern and ask one question that identifies the comparison or underlying risk. Reconnect price to verified value only after the concern is understood.

## Alternatives

- Explain scope differences when the comparison is incomplete.
- Present an approved lower-risk option when one exists.
- Discuss payment structure only when allowed by commercial policy.
- Disqualify respectfully when there is a genuine affordability mismatch.

## Avoid

- Discounting immediately.
- Inventing return on investment.
- Creating false urgency.
- Criticizing a competitor.
- Hiding a public price.

## Internal validation required

No company pricing, discount authority or approved alternatives have been supplied.
